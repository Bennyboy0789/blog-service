5 Signs Your AC Needs Maintenance Before Summer Hits

By Peak Climate Solutions · June 2, 2026 · 4 min read

Nobody wants to be the person whose air conditioner dies on the hottest day of the year. The 3 PM phone call. The "we can get someone out there Thursday" line. The sweat.

Here's the thing: most AC breakdowns don't come out of nowhere. They announce themselves. You just have to know what to listen for. Here are the five signs your system is asking for help before it goes completely silent on you.

1. The airflow feels weak — even on full blast

Stand near a vent. Is the air moving or is it sort of... suggesting itself into the room? Weak airflow usually means one of three things: a clogged filter, a failing blower motor, or ductwork that's leaking cold air into your attic instead of your living room. None of these fix themselves. The filter you can swap yourself in 90 seconds. The other two? That's a pro call.

2. It's making sounds you've never heard before

Air conditioners have a voice. A steady, low hum you stop noticing after a while. What they don't do is screech, rattle, bang, or grind. A screech is often a belt slipping. A rattle could be a loose panel or debris in the fan. A grinding sound — that's metal on metal, and it means turn the system off and pick up the phone. Every hour you run it like that is another hundred dollars of damage.

"80% of the emergency calls we get in June and July are systems that showed warning signs for weeks. The customer just didn't know what to listen for." — Peak Climate service team

3. Your energy bill crept up and you can't explain it

If your July bill is $80 higher than last July and the rates haven't changed, your AC is working harder than it should. A system low on refrigerant runs longer to hit the thermostat setting. A compressor nearing end-of-life draws more power. A dirty coil reduces efficiency. Any one of these shows up in your utility bill before it shows up as a breakdown.

4. One room is a freezer. Another is a sauna.

Inconsistent cooling usually points to a balance issue — dampers that need adjusting, a zoning problem, or a refrigerant level that's just barely adequate. The system cools the easiest rooms first and the farthest rooms last. If the farthest rooms are never getting comfortable, the system is telling you it's struggling.

5. It's been more than a year since anyone looked at it

This one's on you. If you skip inspections, you skip the chance to catch a $200 fix before it becomes a $2,000 replacement. A maintenance visit covers: cleaning the condenser coil, checking refrigerant pressure, tightening electrical connections, inspecting the drain line, and testing the thermostat calibration. Takes about an hour. Costs less than an emergency call. And it extends the life of your system by years.

The bottom line

Your AC isn't going to send you a calendar invite for its funeral. It's going to hum a little louder, leak a little more, and spike your electric bill in ways you'll shrug off until you can't. Pay attention now, or pay more later. That's the deal.
